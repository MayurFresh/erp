Status: research for the labour/wages pack, as of 28 Oct 2026. Every fact has the URL it came from. Anything not confirmed from a source I opened is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

How the sources were read:
- Gazette PDFs were downloaded from egazette.gov.in and read as text.

## 2. Working time and overtime

### Code text
- **Code on Wages s.14.** Work beyond a normal working day is paid "at the overtime rate which shall not be less than twice the normal rate of wages" (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2019/210356.pdf).
- **OSH Code s.25(1).** No worker may work more than "(a) eight hours in a day". Intervals and spread-overs are to be "notified by the appropriate Government" (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2020/222112.pdf).
- **OSH Code s.26(1).** No more than six days in any one week.
- **OSH Code s.27.** Overtime is paid "at the rate of twice the rate of wages". Overtime is "calculated on a daily basis or weekly basis, whichever is more favourable to such worker". Two provisos:
  - "a worker shall be required to work overtime by the employer subject to the consent of such worker"
  - "the appropriate Government may prescribe the total number of hours of overtime"
- **PIB summary** (https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2192524): "Normal working hours capped at 8 hours/day and 48 hours/week. Overtime allowed only with worker consent and paid at twice the regular rate."

### Central rules (the model a state is likely to copy)
- **Code on Wages Rules r.3(2): converting rates.** The daily rate "shall be divided by eight for fixing the rate of wages for an hour and multiplied by twenty six for fixing the rate of wages for a month". Half a rupee or more rounds up; less is dropped (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272365.pdf).
- **r.5: normal working day.** It is eight hours for a daily-wage employee. For anyone else, weekly hours must not exceed 48.
- **r.6: weekly rest day.**
  - Work on the rest day needs a substituted rest day.
  - "no substitution shall be made, which shall result in the employee working for more than ten days consecutively without a rest day".
  - A rest day that is worked is paid at the overtime rate, "not less than twice the normal rate of wages".
- **r.2(1)(p).** "normal rate of wage" means wage as defined in s.2(y). So the overtime base is basic + DA + retaining allowance, not gross pay.
- **OSH Rules r.64.** "No worker shall be required or allowed to work in an establishment for more than forty eight hours in a week" (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272379.pdf).
- **OSH Rules r.69: wages for overtime.**
  - (1) Overtime is due when "(a) a worker works for more than eight hours in any day as daily wager or (b) a worker, other than daily wager, for more than forty-eight hours in any week". It is paid at twice the rate "at the end of each wage period".
  - (2) Rounding: "a fraction of an hour between 15 to 30 minutes shall be counted as thirty minutes and in case of more than thirty minutes it shall be rounded and shall be counted as an hour".
  - (3) For a monthly-paid worker, the daily wage is 1/26 of monthly wages.
  - (4) Hours may exceed the limit for dock, mine and building or other construction work in listed cases: urgent repairs, technical continuity, exceptional press of work and others. The cap: "Provided that no worker shall be allowed to work overtime exceeding one hundred forty-four hours in any quarter of a year".
- **Spread-over and rest intervals.** The central rules do not set them, and the Code on Wages Rules r.5 defers to an OSH notification. That notification, and the Gujarat and Maharashtra values, are **UNVERIFIED**.

## 3. Minimum wages and the floor wage

### Floor wage (Code on Wages s.9)
- **s.9(1).** "The Central Government shall fix floor wage taking into account minimum living standards of a worker". Different floors are allowed for different geographical areas.
- **s.9(2).** State minimum wages "shall not be less than the floor wage" (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2019/210356.pdf).
- **Code on Wages Rules r.10: how it is fixed.**
  - The Centre consults the Central Advisory Board and the States, taking account of "food, clothing, housing and any other factors".
  - It is revised "at an interval not exceeding five years" (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272365.pdf).
- **Rule 3(1): criteria.** Minimum wages are fixed on a day basis "keeping in view the criteria which shall be separately specified by the Central Government by special or general order". The final rules do **not** list the draft norms (3 consumption units, 2,700 calories and so on).
- **Rule 4: VDA revision.** The cost-of-living allowance is recomputed "once before 1st April and then before 1st October in every year", using CPI-IW.
- **Floor wage amount.** I found no notified amount as of Oct 2026. The ₹176 (2017) and ₹178 per day figures in commentary are the old advisory national floor-level minimum wage. Status: **UNVERIFIED / apparently not notified**.

## 4. Social security: EPF and ESI

### EPF wage ceiling is ₹25,000 from 17 Sep 2026 (verified)
- **S.O. 5109(E), 17 Sep 2026.** Gazette CG-DL-E-17092026-276299, https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/276299.pdf. I read the text from a mirror copy: https://www.staffnews.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/MolE-Notification-Wages-Ceiling-17-09-2026.pdf. It reads: "...in supersession of ... S.O. 2702(E) dated 29th May 2026 ... hereby notifies rupees twenty-five thousand (₹25,000) per month as the wage ceiling for the purposes of Chapter III of the said Code, with effect from the date of publication..." (issued under SS Code s.2(89)).
- **Cabinet approval, PIB 16 Sep 2026.** https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2310811: approved enhancing the ceiling "from Rs.15,000 to Rs.25,000 per month".
- **What changes for payroll, PIB 23 Sep 2026.** https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2314111:
  - The contribution wage is "₹25,000 per month or the actual salary/wages, whichever is lower".
  - Employees previously excluded only because their wages were above ₹15,000 now become eligible up to ₹25,000.
  - "the maximum contribution towards EPS shall be calculated at 8.33% of ₹25,000".
- **Employer advisory, PIB Goa 23 Sep 2026.** https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2313790: enrol workers earning ₹15,000–25,000 and update payroll "with effect from September 17, 2026".
- **September 2026 split.** Splitting the month (₹15,000 for 1–16 Sep, ₹25,000 from 17 Sep) is reported only by secondary FAQs: **UNVERIFIED**.

### EPF contribution rates and coverage
- **SS Code s.16(1)(a)** (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2020/222111.pdf):
  - The employer pays 10% of wages, and the employee pays the same. 12% applies to establishments the Centre notifies.
  - The Pension Fund receives "not exceeding eight and one-third per cent. of the wages" out of the employer's share.
- **The common split** (12% + 12%, with the employer's 12% split as 8.33% EPS and 3.67% EPF, plus EDLI 0.5% and admin 0.5%) was not opened: **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Applicability (SS Code First Schedule).**
  - EPF: "Every establishment in which twenty or more employees are employed."
  - ESI: "ten or more persons", or even one person in a notified hazardous occupation.
- **Contract workers (SS Code s.17).**
  - The principal employer may recover contributions from the contractor.
  - The contractor may deduct only the employee's share from wages: "no contractor shall be entitled to deduct the employer's contribution ... from the wages".
- **Wage definition (SS Code s.2(88), same as Code on Wages s.2(y)).**
  - Wages include basic pay, DA and retaining allowance. HRA, conveyance, overtime, commission and the other listed items are excluded.
  - Proviso: if the excluded items (a) to (i) exceed one-half of total remuneration, "the amount which exceeds such one-half ... shall be deemed as remuneration and shall be accordingly added in wages".
  - Sources: https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2019/210356.pdf and PIB https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2192524.

### ESI
- **Wage ceiling.** ESIC Coverage page (https://esic.gov.in/coverage): "effective from 01.01.2017, is Rs.21,000/- per month (Rs.25,000/- per month in the case of Persons with Disability)". I found no 2026 revision.
- **Contribution rates.** SS (Central) Rules r.19 (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272366.pdf):
  - Employer: "three and one-fourth per cent." (3.25%)
  - Employee: "three-fourth per cent." (0.75%)
  - Both are "rounded to the next higher rupee".
- **Low-wage exemption.** ESIC Contribution page (https://www.esic.gov.in/contribution): "Employees in receipt of a daily average wage upto Rs.176/- are exempted from payment of contribution". The employer still pays its share. This exemption does not appear in the SS Central Rules, so whether it continues under the Code is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Contribution and benefit periods** (same ESIC page):

  | Contribution period | Benefit period |
  |---|---|
  | 1 Apr – 30 Sep | 1 Jan – 30 Jun of the next year |
  | 1 Oct – 31 Mar | 1 Jul – 31 Dec |

- **Registration (SS Rules r.18).** The employer must register each person "before or on the day of taking any person into employment", with Aadhaar, on the ESIC portal.
- **Construction site workers.** ESI coverage was extended to construction site workers in implemented areas w.e.f. 01.08.2015. I saw this only in a search snippet of an ESIC RO Odisha notice (https://roodisha.esic.gov.in/attachments/newseventfile/9262d8ee26034f7e7514f595265578f2.pdf) and could not read the PDF: **UNVERIFIED**.

## 5. Building and other construction workers (BOCW)

### Coverage
- **Definition (OSH Code s.2(1)(h)).** Construction, repair, maintenance or demolition of buildings, roads, bridges, pipelines and similar works. It excludes own-residence construction where "the total cost of such work does not exceed rupees fifty lakhs" (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2020/222112.pdf).
- **Threshold (s.2(1)(v)(ii)).** A building or other construction work becomes an "establishment" when "ten or more workers are employed".

### Welfare cess is 1%
- **SS Code s.100(1).** Cess between 1% and 2% of the cost of construction, excluding land.
- **S.O. 2322(E), 8 May 2026** (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272359.pdf): "the Central Government hereby specifies a cess at the rate of one per cent. of the cost of construction incurred by an employer on building or other construction work". It supersedes S.O. 2899 of 1996.
- **SS Code s.103.** The final cess is paid on self-assessment within 60 days of completion of each work, net of advance cess.
- **SS (Central) Rules r.41–42: procedure** (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272366.pdf):
  - Form XV goes to the assessing officer within 60 days of commencement.
  - Advance cess is paid on a self-assessment "duly certified by a chartered engineer", using PWD, CPWD or RERA cost rates (Form XVI).
  - Where a local authority approves the plan, proof of cess payment goes with the approval application. If the project runs longer than a year, the first payment covers one year's cost.
  - Government and PSU clients deduct cess from bills and deposit it within 30 days.
  - Stoppage or reduction is reported in Form XVII within 60 days. The completion return, Form XVIII, is due within 60 days of completion.
  - Scrutiny happens only if self-assessed cess exceeds ₹10 lakh. If no order is passed within 180 days, the self-assessment is deemed final.
  - Late payment carries interest at "one percent per month or part thereof".

### Worker registration
- **SS Code s.106.** Workers aged 18 to under 60 who were engaged for "not less than ninety days during the preceding twelve months" are registered as beneficiaries.
- **s.107.** Registration lapses at age 60 or when the worker has fewer than 90 days in a year.
- **SS Rules r.45: duties of the employer or contractor.**
  - Register eligible workers on the muster roll on the State Board or central portal "within 30 days of the worker(s) becoming eligible", seeded with Aadhaar and UAN.
  - When a worker moves between States, update the destination State's portal within 30 days of the worker joining.
  - Welfare benefits are paid by direct benefit transfer only.
- **Not verified:** Gujarat and Maharashtra BOCW Board registration fees, and the e-Shram linkage.

### Other OSH duties for construction sites
OSH (Central) Rules, https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272379.pdf:
- **r.4.** A BOCW or contract-labour establishment files a notice of commencement or cessation in Form VI within 30 days. A cessation notice needs a certificate that all dues to workers have been paid.
- **r.5.** Free annual medical examination for building workers who are 40 or older.
- **r.62.** Free temporary living accommodation at sites far from workers' homes. "the principal employer will pay the expenses incurred on providing accommodation to the contractor where the building or other construction work is done through the contractor."

## 6. Contract labour (OSH Code Part I of Chapter XI)

### Code text
OSH Code, https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2020/222112.pdf:
- **Registration, s.3.** Every establishment registers electronically within 60 days. If the officer does not act, registration is deemed and auto-generated.
- **s.45(1).** The contract labour Part applies to "every establishment in which fifty or more contract labour are employed or were employed on any day of the preceding twelve months" and to manpower suppliers with 50 or more.
- **s.47.** Contractors need a licence. A "work specific licence" is available where the contractor does not meet the criteria. A proviso allows a single licence "in more than one States or for the whole of India" from the central authority.
- **s.48(3).** The licence is valid for five years.
- **s.49.** The contractor must not charge workers any fee or commission.
- **s.50.** The contractor must intimate each work order.
- **s.53.** The **principal employer** provides the s.23 and s.24 welfare facilities to contract labour.
- **s.54.** Using a contractor who should be licensed but is not is a contravention by the principal employer.
- **s.55: wages.**
  - The contractor pays wages "through bank transfer or electronic mode and inform the principal employer electronically".
  - (3) If the contractor fails or short-pays, "the principal employer shall be liable to make payment of the wages in full or the unpaid balance due ... and recover the amount so paid from the contractor".
- **s.57.** Contract labour in core activities is prohibited. Exceptions: work ordinarily done through contractors, work that does not need full-time workers, or a sudden surge in volume.
- **s.59–61.** Applies to establishments with 10 or more inter-State migrant workers. The employer pays a yearly lump-sum to-and-fro fare to the worker's native place.

### Central rules
OSH (Central) Rules, https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272379.pdf:
- **r.3: registration.** Form I on the Shram Suvidha portal. The certificate (Form III) is issued within 7 days, failing which it is deemed issued. Establishments already registered must update within six months.
- **r.6: appointment letter.** Mandatory before employment. Fields: name, DOB, parent's name, Aadhaar (with consent), establishment LIN, UAN/ESIC IP no., designation, employment type (regular / fixed-term / contractual), skill category, date of joining, wages (basic + DA), other allowances, EPF/ESI applicability, nature of duties.
- **r.86 and r.89: licence conditions.** Hours per s.25. Wages per the Code on Wages, never below notified rates. Toilets, drinking water, first aid, canteen and crèche are provided by the principal employer when contract labour works at its premises.
- **r.88: single multi-State licence.** Applied for on Shram Suvidha. Auto-generated if not decided in 45 days. Valid five years.
- **r.90: security and fees.**
  - Security is a bank guarantee of "Rs. 1000/- for each of the contract labours".
  - Fees: "No license is required up to 49 contract labour". ₹1,000 for 50–100, ₹2,000 for 101–300, ₹3,000 for 301–500, ₹5,000 for 501–1,000, rising to ₹40,000 for more than 20,000.
- **r.93: equal treatment.**
  - Contract labour doing "the same or similar kind of work" as direct workers gets the same holidays, hours and conditions.
  - "All contract labour shall be made member of Employees' Provident Fund Organisation and Employees' State Insurance Corporation subject to applicability".
- **r.94.** The contractor intimates each work order within 15 days on Shram Suvidha: principal employer, site, start date, headcount and duration.
- **r.98: contractor wages.**
  - The wage period must not exceed one month.
  - Wages are paid "before the expiry of seventh day after the last day of the wage period", by bank or electronic transfer, without deductions other than those specified.
  - A notice of the wage period and payment date is displayed and copied to the principal employer.
  - The contractor files a half-yearly return in Form XVIII.
  - (8) If the contractor does not pay within 7 days, the principal employer pays "within fifteen days" and recovers from the contractor's bills or security.
  - The principal employer files an annual return in Form XVII Part III by end of February.
- **r.102: migrant journey allowance.** Fare by "II Class sleeper or by bus" to the home State, if the worker has 180 or more days in the preceding 12 months. Paid once in 12 months.
- **Code on Wages (Central) Rules.**
  - r.11: the proprietor pays the contractor the wage amounts due under the Code.
  - r.21: if the contractor fails to pay bonus, the principal employer pays the minimum bonus (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272365.pdf).
  - PIB: "If the contractor fails to pay wages, the principal employer has to pay unpaid wages to the contract labour" (https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2192524).

## 7. Records, payment timelines and deductions

### Wage period and payment deadlines
Code on Wages, https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2019/210356.pdf:
- **s.16.** The wage period is daily, weekly, fortnightly or monthly. "no wage period in respect of any employee shall be more than a month".
- **s.17(1): payment deadlines.**
  - Daily: "at the end of the shift"
  - Weekly: "on the last working day of the week, that is to say, before the weekly holiday"
  - Fortnightly: "before the end of the second day after the end of the fortnight"
  - Monthly: "before the expiry of the seventh day of the succeeding month"
- **s.17(2).** On removal, dismissal, retrenchment, resignation or closure, wages are paid "within two working days".
- **s.15.** Wages are paid in coin or notes, by cheque, by bank credit or electronically.

### Deductions (s.18–24 and Code on Wages Rules r.13–20)
- **s.18(2): permitted purposes, and no others.**
  - fines
  - absence from duty
  - damage or loss due to the worker's neglect
  - housing
  - amenities
  - advances (including travel advances) and overpayment adjustment
  - welfare-fund loans and approved housing loans
  - income tax and statutory levies
  - PF, pension and ESI
  - co-operative society
  - trade-union fees (with written authority)
  - PM's National Relief Fund (with written authority)
- **s.18(3).** Total deductions in a wage period "shall not exceed fifty per cent. of such wages".
- **r.13.** Any excess "shall be carried forward and recovered from the wages of succeeding wage period, in instalments" so that no month's recovery exceeds 50% (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272365.pdf).
- **Fines (s.19).**
  - Fines are allowed only for acts and omissions listed in a notice approved by the authority. Under the central rules (r.14) that authority is the Deputy Chief Labour Commissioner; r.15 requires the notice to be displayed in Hindi, English and the local language.
  - The worker must get a chance to show cause. r.16 allows seven days, and the worker is told within 15 days of imposition.
  - s.19(4): the total "shall not exceed an amount equal to three per cent. of the wages payable to him in respect of that wage-period".
  - No fine on a worker under 15.
  - No recovery "by instalments or after the expiry of ninety days".
  - Fines go into a register, and the proceeds are used only for worker welfare.
- **Absence (s.20).** The deduction is proportional to the period absent. If ten or more workers absent themselves in concert without notice, up to 8 days' wages may be deducted. Under r.17 the worker gets 7 days to reply.
- **Damage or loss (s.21, r.18).** The deduction must not exceed the actual loss. The worker gets 7 days to show cause and is told within 15 days.
- **Advances (s.23, r.19).**
  - An advance given before employment is recovered from the first full wage period. Advances for travelling expenses are not recovered.
  - Advances after joining, and advances of unearned wages, are recovered "in instalments determined by the employer, so as any or all instalments in a wage period, shall not exceed fifty per cent of the wages", and are recorded in Form IV.
- **Loans (s.24, r.20).** Follow the Central Government's guidelines on amount and interest.

### Registers, payslip and muster roll
Code on Wages (Central) Rules r.51–52, forms at https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272365.pdf:
- **What to keep.** Kept "electronically or in physical form", preserved for **five years** after the last entry:
  - Form I, Employee Register
  - Form IV, Register of Wages, Overtime, Advances, Fines and Deductions for Damage and Loss
  - Form IX, Attendance Register-cum-Muster Roll
- **Payslip.** Issued in Form V "on or before payment of wages".
- **Form I fields.**
  - Header: establishment, employer, owner, PAN/TAN, LIN.
  - Employee: employee code, name, surname, gender, father's/mother's/spouse name, DOB, place of birth, nationality, education level, date of joining, designation, category (HS/S/SS/US), employment type (Permanent/Temporary/Fixed Term/Trainee/Badli), posting, pay, promotion, mobile, UAN, PAN, nominee, family details, EPS/NPS, ESIC IP no., Aadhaar, bank a/c, bank, branch (IFSC), present and permanent address, service book no., date of exit, reason for exit, identification mark, photo, signature or thumb impression, remarks.
- **Form IV columns.**
  - sl no; employee code; name; designation; department
  - payment basis (monthly/fortnightly/weekly/daily/piece-rated); wage period from–to
  - days worked; overtime hours (or overtime production for piece workers)
  - rates (basic, DA, allowances); earned (basic, DA, allowances, overtime, total)
  - deductions (EPF, ESIC, society, income tax, insurance, advances, fine recovery, damage/loss recovery, others, total); net payment
  - date of payment; employee receipt or bank transaction ID
  - fine details (act or omission with date, amount); damage or loss caused; signature (physical registers only)
- **Form V payslip.** Date of issue, establishment, address, period, employee name, parent's or spouse's name, designation, UAN, bank a/c, wage period, rate (basic, DA, allowances), attendance or units of work, overtime wages, gross wages, deductions (PF, ESI, others), net paid.
- **Form IX muster roll.** Per month: employee code, name, designation, shift, place of work or section. In and out time for each day 1–31, signature, total days worked, total overtime hours, tour details.
- **OSH (Central) Rules r.72.**
  - Registers kept under the Code on Wages are "deemed to be maintained" for OSH.
  - Records are kept at the workplace or within 3 km.
  - Annual return in Forms XVII and XVIII by 28/29 February.
  - r.73: the notice board shows hours of work, wage period and payment date (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272379.pdf).
- **SS (Central) Rules r.53.** The same Code on Wages Forms I, IX and IV and payslip Form V are reused, plus a women-employee register in Form XXII (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/272366.pdf).

### Bonus and gratuity (brief)
- **Bonus (Code on Wages s.26).** A minimum bonus of "eight and one-third per cent. of the wages earned ... or one hundred rupees, whichever is higher" for an employee with at least 30 days' work in the accounting year. The maximum is 20%.
  - Eligibility and calculation ceilings are "as determined by notification". The values under the Code (₹21,000 / ₹7,000 under the old Act) are **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Gratuity for fixed-term employees.** IR Code s.2(o)(c): "he shall be eligible for gratuity if he renders service under the contract for a period of one year" (https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2020/222118.pdf). PIB: "eligible for gratuity after one year of continuous service (earlier five years)".

## 8. What Progen needs to build

Progen already has workers, attendance with OT, and wage sheets (OT = otHours × dailyRate ÷ 8 × 2, plus advance recovery). The gaps are below.

### 1. Rate master by state, zone, skill and period
- Store basic and VDA separately, with valid-from and valid-to dates. VDA changes every 1 Apr / 1 Oct in Gujarat and 1 Jan / 1 Jul in Maharashtra.
- Each site carries state + zone (Gujarat Zone 1 or 2; Maharashtra Zone I, II or III), from which the applicable minimum wage is resolved.
- Warn or block when a worker's daily rate is below basic + VDA for their skill.

### 2. Split the wage into components
- Use basic, DA/VDA, retaining allowance, HRA, conveyance and other allowances.
- Compute statutory "wages" with the 50% add-back. This one figure drives the OT base, PF and ESI wages, bonus and gratuity.

### 3. OT engine
- Keep the formula `otHours × (wageRate ÷ 8) × 2`, but use *wages* (basic + DA), not the gross daily rate, when the rate includes HRA or allowances. Round per Code on Wages Rules r.3(2) and OSH Rules r.69.
- Daily wagers: OT after 8 h per day. Others: after 48 h per week, using daily or weekly, whichever is more favourable.
- Rounding: 15–30 min counts as 0.5 h; more than 30 min counts as 1 h.
- Rest-day work is paid at the OT rate, and a substitute rest day is tracked. Alert when a worker reaches 10 consecutive days without rest.
- Quarterly OT cap of **144 h**, with a warning at 80%.
- A worker-consent flag on OT entries.

### 4. Attendance
- In/out time per day, shift and place of work, so Form IX can be generated directly.
- A weekly-off master and a six-day-per-week check.

### 5. Pay calendar
- Wage period per worker or site (daily, weekly, fortnightly or monthly; never more than a month).
- Due-date calculation per s.17, and 7 days after the period end for contract labour.
- An "F&F within 2 working days" alert on exit.
- Overdue-wage dashboard.

### 6. Deductions engine
- Typed deductions limited to the s.18 list.
- Hard cap at 50% of wages per period, with the excess carried forward automatically (r.13).
- Advances: separate pre-employment and post-joining advances. Travel advances are not recoverable. Instalment schedule with 50% headroom.
- Fines module:
  - approved acts/omissions list
  - show-cause record (7 days) and intimation (15 days)
  - ≤3% of period wages
  - no fines on workers under 15
  - recover in one go within 90 days
  - fine-fund ledger
- Damage/loss: show-cause record, capped at the actual loss.

### 7. Statutory registers and payslip export
- Form I, Form IV, Form IX and the Form V payslip, as PDF and Excel.
- Electronic payslip issued on or before the pay date.
- Five-year retention and no hard delete.

### 8. Worker master fields
Everything in Form I plus the appointment-letter fields (OSH r.6):
- Aadhaar (with a consent flag), UAN, ESIC IP no., bank/IFSC, skill category, employment type, nominee, family
- date of birth, used for the age 18–60 BOCW eligibility and the 40+ annual medical
- home state, used for the migrant flag
- BOCW Board registration no. and expiry

### 9. Appointment letter generator
In the OSH r.6 format, with a block on wage processing when no letter has been issued.

### 10. PF and ESI
- PF ceiling configurable, defaulting to **₹25,000 from 17 Sep 2026** (₹15,000 before), with the September 2026 split-month logic.
- EPS at 8.33% of min(wages, ceiling).
- ESI at 3.25% / 0.75%, rounded up to the next rupee, with the ₹21,000 ceiling (₹25,000 for persons with disability). Coverage continues to the end of the contribution period.
- The ₹176-per-day employee exemption kept as a configurable rule.
- Applicability flags by headcount: EPF 20+, ESI 10+.
- ECR and ESIC monthly export files.

### 11. Contractor (labour) module
- Contractor master: licence no., validity (5 years), licensed headcount, security amount.
- Licence-required check when contract headcount on any day in the last 12 months is 50 or more.
- Work-order intimation date (within 15 days).
- Contractor wage-payment proof (bank file / UTR), checked against the 7-day deadline. If unpaid, raise a principal-employer payment within 15 days and recover it from RA bills or retention. Link to `docs/research/contractor-billing.md`.
- Check that contract workers doing the same work as direct workers get equal rates.

### 12. BOCW
- Per-project cess: estimated cost (PWD/CPWD/RERA rates), 1% advance cess paid, CE certificate, Form XV / XVI / XVII / XVIII dates.
- Final self-assessment within 60 days of completion.
- Interest at 1% per month on late payment.
- Worker registration tracker: flag a worker on reaching 90 days in 12 months, with registration due within 30 days of eligibility.
- Site commencement and cessation notice (Form VI, within 30 days).
- Accommodation and medical-check flags.

### 13. Inter-State migrants
- Flag workers whose home state differs from the site state, and count them per establishment (10 or more triggers the Part).
- Journey-allowance eligibility at 180 days in 12 months, once a year.

### 14. Annual returns
Data for Form XVII and XVIII (OSH) and Form XXIII (SS) by end of February. The contractor's half-yearly return goes in Form XVIII.
