## 1. Unit inventory and booking flow

**Sell by carpet area.** RERA s.2(k): "'carpet area' means the net usable floor area of an apartment, excluding the area covered by the external walls, areas under services shafts, exclusive balcony or verandah area and exclusive open terrace area, but includes the area covered by the internal partition walls of the apartment." Source: RERA Act.
- Balconies, verandahs and open terraces for exclusive use are separate fields. They are not part of the carpet area (s.2(k), Explanation). Source: RERA Act.

**Area variation after OC (Maharashtra model agreement, cl. 1(g)).** The final carpet area is confirmed after the occupancy certificate, "subject to a variation cap of three percent".
- If the area shrinks, the promoter "shall refund the excess money paid by Allottee within forty-five days with annual interest at the rate specified in the Rules".
- If the area grows, the extra amount is demanded "as per the next milestone of the Payment Plan".
- Both adjustments are at "the same rate per square meter" as agreed.
- Source: MH Rules 2017, Annexure.

**Parking.**
- Open parking is a common area. s.2(n)(iii): common areas include "the common basements, terraces, parks, play areas, open parking areas". So open parking cannot be sold as a unit. Source: RERA Act.
- s.2(y): a "garage" is "a place within a project having a roof and walls on three sides for parking any vehicle, but does not include an unenclosed or uncovered parking space such as open parking areas". Source: RERA Act.
- MH Rules r.2(j) defines a "covered parking space" as an enclosed or covered area in basement, stilt, podium or mechanised parking, which "shall not include a garage and/or open parking". Source: MH Rules 2017.
- The model agreement cl. 1(a)(ii) and (iii) sells garages and covered parking spaces each "for the consideration of Rs. ___". It notes that the apartment price including "parking spaces should be shown separately". Source: MH Rules 2017.

**What the promoter must publish and update every quarter.** s.11(1) requires the following on the RERA web page:
- (b) "quarterly up-to-date the list of number and types of apartments or plots, as the case may be, booked"
- (c) "quarterly up-to-date the list of number of garages booked"
- (d) "quarterly up-to-date the list of approvals taken and the approvals which are pending subsequent to commencement certificate"
- (e) "quarterly up-to-date status of the project"
- Source: RERA Act.

**Disclosures at booking.** s.11(3): "at the time of the booking and issue of allotment letter", the promoter must make available the sanctioned plans, layout plans and specifications, plus "the stage wise time schedule of completion of the project". Source: RERA Act.

**No mortgage after the agreement.** s.11(4)(h): after executing an agreement for sale, the promoter must "not mortgage or create a charge on such apartment". Source: RERA Act.

**Promoter cancellation.** s.11(5): "The promoter may cancel the allotment only in terms of the agreement for sale". The allottee may approach the Authority if the cancellation is "unilateral and without any sufficient cause". Source: RERA Act.

## 2. Agreement for sale and the 10% cap

**The 10% cap.** s.13(1): "A promoter shall not accept a sum more than ten per cent of the cost of the apartment, plot, or building ... as an advance payment or an application fee, from a person without first entering into a written agreement for sale with such person and register the said agreement for sale". Source: RERA Act.

**What the agreement must contain.** s.13(2): the agreement must specify "the dates and the manner by which payments ... are to be made", "the date on which the possession ... is to be handed over", and "the rates of interest payable by the promoter to the allottee and the allottee to the promoter in case of default". Source: RERA Act.

**Same cap in the Maharashtra model agreement.** Cl. 1(c) records an advance "(not exceeding 10% of the total consideration)". Source: MH Rules 2017.

**Allottee who does not execute (Maharashtra model agreement, cl. 18).**
- The allottee must sign and return the agreement with the payments due "within 30 (thirty) days from the date of receipt".
- If not, the promoter serves a notice. If the default is "not rectified within 15 (fifteen) days ... application of the Allottee shall be treated as cancelled".
- All sums, "including the booking amount shall be returned to the Allottee without any interest or compensation whatsoever".
- Source: MH Rules 2017.

**Price excludes taxes; escalation only for government levies.**
- Cl. 1(d): the price excludes taxes.
- Cl. 1(e): the price is "escalation-free" except for increases in government or development charges. When raising such a demand the promoter "shall enclose the said notification/order/rule/regulation ... along with the demand letter". The increase is applicable "only ... on subsequent payments".
- Source: MH Rules 2017.

**Later MahaRERA revisions.** The model agreement above is the 2017 Annexure. MahaRERA has since issued orders and circulars with mandatory clauses and standard allotment-letter formats. **UNVERIFIED**: the current consolidated version and any cancellation-deduction cap set by MahaRERA orders. Check https://www.maharera.maharashtra.gov.in/circular.

## 3. Construction-linked payment plan and demand letters

**Stage caps (Maharashtra model agreement, cl. 1(c)).** Each cap is cumulative, as a share of total consideration. Source: MH Rules 2017.

| Stage | Cumulative cap |
| --- | --- |
| Advance / application before agreement | ≤10% |
| After execution of agreement | ≤30% |
| Completion of plinth of the building/wing | ≤45% |
| Completion of slabs, including podiums and stilts | ≤70% |
| Walls, internal plaster, floorings, doors and windows of the apartment | ≤75% |
| Sanitary fittings, staircases, lift wells, lobbies up to the apartment's floor | ≤80% |
| External plumbing, external plaster, elevation, terraces with waterproofing | ≤85% |
| Lifts, water pumps, electrical fittings, electro-mechanical and environment requirements, entrance lobby, plinth protection, paving | ≤95% |
| Balance at handing over of possession, "on or after receipt of occupancy certificate or completion certificate" | 100% |

- Early-payment rebate (cl. 1(f)): the promoter may discount early instalments "@ % per annum". Once granted, it is not subject to "revision/withdrawal". Source: MH Rules 2017.
- Other demands (allottee covenants, item vii; clause number not confirmed): the allottee pays "within fifteen days of demand by the Promoter" their share of security deposits for water, electricity and other service connections. Source: MH Rules 2017.

**Default and termination (cl. 4.2).**
- Interest under cl. 4.1 runs on any late amount.
- "on the allottee committing three defaults of payment of instalments, the Promoter shall at his own option, may terminate this Agreement".
- Notice: "Promoter shall give notice of fifteen days in writing to the Allottee, by Registered Post AD ... and mail at the e-mail address ... of his intention to terminate this Agreement and of the specific breach or breaches". Termination takes effect if the breach is not rectified within the notice period.
- Refund: "within a period of thirty days of the termination", the instalments paid are refunded, "subject to adjustment and recovery of any agreed liquidated damages".
- Source: MH Rules 2017.

**Statutory duty to pay on schedule.**
- s.19(6): the allottee must pay "in the manner and within the time as specified in the said agreement for sale", plus their share of registration charges, municipal taxes, water and electricity charges, maintenance and similar charges.
- s.19(7): interest is payable on delay.
- Source: RERA Act.

## 4. Interest on delay (SBI MCLR + 2%)

**One rate both ways.** s.2(za), Explanation (i): "the rate of interest chargeable from the allottee by the promoter, in case of default, shall be equal to the rate of interest which the promoter shall be liable to pay the allottee, in case of default".

**When interest starts and stops.** s.2(za), Explanation (ii):
- Promoter-to-allottee interest runs "from the date the promoter received the amount ... till the date the amount ... and interest thereon is refunded".
- Allottee-to-promoter interest runs "from the date the allottee defaults in payment ... till the date it is paid".
- Source: RERA Act.

**Delay in possession.**
- s.18(1): if possession is delayed and the allottee withdraws, the promoter refunds the money "with interest at such rate as may be prescribed", plus compensation.
- Proviso: an allottee who does not withdraw "shall be paid, by the promoter, interest for every month of delay, till the handing over of the possession".
- s.19(4) gives the matching refund right.
- Source: RERA Act.

**Maharashtra, rule 18.** "The rate of interest payable by the promoters to the allottees or by the allottees to the promoters, as the case may be, shall be the State Bank of India highest Marginal Cost of Lending Rate plus two percent". Proviso: "in case the State Bank of India Marginal Cost of Lending Rate is not in use it would be replaced by such benchmark lending rates which the State Bank of India may fix from time to time for lending to the general public." Source: MH Rules 2017.

**Maharashtra, rule 19.** Refunds with interest and compensation are due "within thirty days from the date on which such refund ... becomes due and payable". Each instance "shall be reported by the concerned promoters within thirty days to the Authority". Source: MH Rules 2017.

**Current rate.**
- SBI MCLR "With Effect From 15th October 2026": Overnight 7.85, 1M 7.85, 3M 8.40, 6M 8.75, 1Y 8.85, 2Y 8.90, **3Y 8.95** (highest tenor). "Last Updated On : Thursday, 15-10-2026". Re-checked 28 Oct 2026. Source: SBI MCLR.
- So the Maharashtra rule-18 rate is **10.95% p.a.** from 15 Oct 2026. Progen must store this as a dated rate table, not a constant.

**Gujarat, rule 16 of the Gujarat RERA (General) Rules 2017.** **UNVERIFIED.** The official text could not be opened (gujrera.gujarat.gov.in/Acts returned 404). Secondary sources say rule 16 sets a contractual rate "as may be mutually agreed" between promoter and allottee, defaulting to SBI MCLR + 2%. Its wording differs from Maharashtra's "highest MCLR". Confirm the exact text before coding the Gujarat rate.

## 5. The 70% separate account and withdrawals

**The rule.** RERA s.4(2)(l)(D): "seventy per cent. of the amounts realised for the real estate project from the allottees, from time to time, shall be deposited in a separate account to be maintained in a scheduled bank to cover the cost of construction and the land cost and shall be used only for that purpose". Its provisos say:
- "the promoter shall withdraw the amounts from the separate account, to cover the cost of the project, in proportion to the percentage of completion of the project"
- withdrawal is allowed only "after it is certified by an engineer, an architect and a chartered accountant in practice that the withdrawal is in proportion to the percentage of completion of the project"
- "the promoter shall get his accounts audited within six months after the end of every financial year by a chartered accountant in practice", and the audit verifies that project money was used for the project and that withdrawals followed the completion proportion
- Source: RERA Act.

**Maharashtra, rule 5(1)(i)(b).** Every withdrawal needs three certificates submitted "to the scheduled bank operating the separate account":
- "First, from the project Architect certifying the percentage of completion of construction work of each of the building / wing of the project"
- "Second, a certificate from the Engineer for the actual cost incurred on the construction work of each of the building / wing"
- "Third, a certificate from a practicing Chartered Accountant, for the cost incurred on construction cost and the land cost". The CA "shall also certify the proportion of the cost incurred on construction and land cost to the total estimated cost of the project. The total estimated cost of the project multiplied by such proportion shall determine the maximum amount which can be withdrawn".
- This applies "at the time of every withdrawal ... till occupancy certificate". After the completion certificate, "the entire balance amount lying in the separate account can be withdrawn".
- Source: MH Rules 2017.

**Withdrawal formula Progen should implement:**
- max cumulative withdrawal = total estimated project cost × (land cost + construction cost incurred, per CA) ÷ (total estimated cost)
- available now = max cumulative withdrawal − amounts already withdrawn

**MahaRERA form numbers.** In practice the three certificates are filed as Form 1 (Architect), Form 2 (Engineer) and Form 3 (CA), with a CA annual audit report as Form 5. **UNVERIFIED.** The numbering and current formats come from MahaRERA circulars that were not opened in this pass. The certificate contents above come from rule 5.

**Gujarat.** **UNVERIFIED.** Secondary news reports say that from 1 Jan 2025 a single new Form 8 replaced Forms 1, 2 and 3 for GujRERA progress updates. Source: https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MTMzODU=/gujrera-simplifies-quarterly-reporting-with-new-form-8-effective-january-2025 (not opened).

## 6. Quarterly progress reports (QPR)

**Statutory basis.**
- RERA s.11(1)(b)–(e): quarterly updates of apartments booked, garages booked, approvals taken and pending, and project status. See §1. Source: RERA Act.
- Maharashtra rule 20(2): the Authority "shall ensure that such information is updated at the interval of every quarter". Source: MH Rules 2017.
- Promoter's declaration prescribed in the MH Rules 2017 (believed to be Form B; form letter not confirmed), item 8: the promoter must tell the Authority about changes to the registration information "within seven days of the said changes occurring". Source: MH Rules 2017.

**Maharashtra QPR mechanics.** **UNVERIFIED.** The fields, due dates and penalties for late QPR come from MahaRERA circulars and orders, which were not opened. Expect the QPR to carry:
- inventory booked and sold
- construction progress by building or wing
- approvals
- Forms 1/2/3
- separate-account details

Current circular list: https://www.maharera.maharashtra.gov.in/circular

**Gujarat QPR.** Secondary only, **UNVERIFIED** against GujRERA. Source opened: DeshGujarat news, 20 Dec 2024, https://deshgujarat.com/2024/12/20/gujrera-introduces-daily-late-fee-for-delayed-quarterly-progress-reports-from-january-2025/
- Filing windows: "January 1-7, April 1-7, July 1-7, and October 1-7 each year".
- Before 2025, a missed deadline locked the QPR and triggered a ₹10,000 late processing fee.
- From 1 Jan 2025, late filers "will not have their QPR locked but will instead be subject to a daily late fee based on the project's cost". The fee is ₹400–₹1,000 per day depending on project cost.
- Backlog: "Only after settling these fees will the builder be allowed to upload the report for the new quarter."

## 7. Cancellation and refund rules

| Trigger | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Promoter cancels | Only "in terms of the agreement for sale"; the allottee can challenge a unilateral cancellation (s.11(5)) | RERA Act |
| Allottee defaults | 3 instalment defaults, then 15-day written notice (RPAD and email), then termination. Refund within 30 days, less agreed liquidated damages (MH model cl. 4.2) | MH Rules 2017 |
| Allottee does not sign or register the agreement | 30 days to sign, then 15-day notice, then the application is cancelled. Full refund including the booking amount, no interest (MH model cl. 18) | MH Rules 2017 |
| Promoter delays possession or registration is revoked; allottee withdraws | Full refund with prescribed interest plus compensation (s.18(1), s.19(4)) | RERA Act |
| Misleading advertisement or model flat; allottee withdraws | "entire investment along with interest" plus compensation (s.12) | RERA Act |
| Carpet area shrinks after OC (within 3%) | Refund within 45 days with interest (MH model cl. 1(g)) | MH Rules 2017 |
| Any refund due (Maharashtra) | Pay within 30 days; report to MahaRERA within 30 days (r.19) | MH Rules 2017 |

**Voluntary cancellation by the allottee.** **UNVERIFIED.** No forfeiture cap appears in the Act or in the 2017 Maharashtra model agreement. Any cap (for example a percentage of agreement value) would come from later MahaRERA orders, allotment-letter formats or case law. Make deductions a configurable policy that defaults to the agreement's liquidated-damages clause.

## 8. GST on flat sales

**Rates under Notification 11/2017-CT(Rate), Sl. No. 3, heading 9954, as amended by 03/2019-CT(Rate) w.e.f. 1 Apr 2019.** The CGST rates are below; SGST/UTGST is the same again. Source: GST N.11/2017.

| Item | Supply | CGST | Gross GST | Effective on total price after 1/3 land deduction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (i) | "Construction of affordable residential apartments by a promoter in a Residential Real Estate Project (RREP)" | 0.75% | 1.5% | **1%** |
| (ia) | "Construction of residential apartments other than affordable residential apartments" in an RREP | 3.75% | 7.5% | **5%** |
| (ib) | "Construction of commercial apartments (shops, offices, godowns etc.)" in an RREP | 3.75% | 7.5% | 5% |

**Conditions.**
- "central tax at the rate specified ... shall be paid in cash, that is, by debiting the electronic cash ledger only".
- "credit of input tax charged on goods and services used in supplying the service has not been taken". There are Annexure I/II reversal rules for ongoing projects.
- Source: GST N.11/2017.

**Land deduction, para 2.** The value of land or undivided share of land "shall be deemed to be one third of the total amount charged for such supply". So tax applies on 2/3 of the total amount. Source: GST N.11/2017.

**Worked examples.**
- Non-affordable, ₹50,00,000 total: taxable ₹33,33,333; CGST 3.75% = ₹1,25,000; SGST ₹1,25,000; total GST ₹2,50,000 (5%).
- Affordable, ₹40,00,000: taxable ₹26,66,667; CGST 0.75% = ₹20,000; SGST ₹20,000; total GST ₹40,000 (1%).

**Affordable residential apartment (para 4, Explanation (xvi)).** "carpet area not exceeding 60 square meter in metropolitan cities or 90 square meter in cities or towns other than metropolitan cities and for which the gross amount charged is not more than forty five lakhs rupees".
- Metro cities: "Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi NCR (limited to Delhi, Noida, Greater Noida, Ghaziabad, Gurgaon, Faridabad), Hyderabad, Kolkata and Mumbai (whole of MMR)".
- The "gross amount" for the ₹45 lakh test includes the construction consideration, the land / undivided share (including lease), and "any other amount charged by the promoter from the buyer".
- Source: GST N.11/2017.

**No GST after the completion certificate.** Items (i), (ia) and (ib) apply to apartments "intended for sale to a buyer, wholly or partly, except where the entire consideration has been received after issuance of completion certificate, where required, by the competent authority or after its first occupation, whichever is earlier". Source: GST N.11/2017.
- The underlying law is CGST Act Schedule II para 5(b) and Schedule III para 5 ("sale of building", subject to Schedule II para 5(b), is neither a supply of goods nor of services). **UNVERIFIED** in this pass; the Act text was not opened.
- Progen rule: if any instalment was received before the CC or first occupation, GST applies to the instalments. If the entire consideration is received after the CC, no GST.

**GST 2.0 (rate rationalisation w.e.f. 22 Sep 2025, Notification 15/2025-CT(Rate) dated 17 Sep 2025).** **UNVERIFIED.** Secondary sources say the residential 1%/5% no-ITC entries were not changed. The official CBIC text (https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/view-pdf/1010453/ENG/Notifications) loads through JavaScript and could not be read.

## 9. TDS on property purchase (Income-tax Act 2025)

Verified by the coordinating agent; not re-opened in this pass. Its sources are listed in `docs/research/contractor-billing.md`: the PRS copy of the Act and the Income-tax Rules 2026 gazette mirror.

**Property TDS (replaces old s.194-IA).** s.393(1) Table Sl. No. 3(i): **1% of the higher of consideration and stamp duty value**, where that value is **≥ ₹50 lakh**. The buyer deducts.

**Forms.** **Form 141** is the challan-cum-statement, due within 30 days from the end of the month of deduction. **Form 132** is the TDS certificate. Together they replace old Form 26QB and Form 16B.

**Commission or brokerage (replaces old s.194H).** s.393(1) Sl. No. 1(ii): 2%, threshold ₹20,000.

**No PAN.** The higher rate applies, 20% (s.397(2)(b)(i)), per `contractor-billing.md`.

**Old-law amendment, not re-verified.** The Finance Act 2022 put club membership, car parking, electricity/water facility, maintenance, advance and similar incidental charges inside "consideration". **UNVERIFIED** whether the 2025 Act carries the same wording.

## 11. Brokers and agents (RERA s.9)

- **Registration is mandatory.** s.9(1): no real estate agent may facilitate the sale of a unit in a registered project "without obtaining registration under this section". Source: RERA Act.
- **Who counts as an agent.** s.2(zm) "includes property dealers, brokers, middlemen by whatever name called" who receive "commission or otherwise". Source: RERA Act.
- **Quote the number.** s.9(5): the registration number "shall be quoted by the real estate agent in every sale facilitated by him". Source: RERA Act.
- **Agent duties.** s.10: deal only in registered projects, keep books and records, avoid unfair trade practices. Source: RERA Act.
- **Penalty.** s.62: ₹10,000 per day of default, up to 5% of the unit cost. Source: RERA Act.
- **Maharashtra fees and validity.**
  - r.11(3): fee of "rupees ten thousand" for an individual and "rupees one lakh" for others.
  - r.13: renew at least 60 days before expiry; renewal valid 5 years.
  - r.14: display the registration number, and quote it together with the project's registration number on all advertising and sale documents.
  - Source: MH Rules 2017.
- **Maharashtra Certificate of Competency.** **UNVERIFIED** (secondary source only): MahaRERA Notification No. MahaRERA/Secy/File No. 27/1594/2023 dated 13 Dec 2023. From 1 Jan 2024, agents need a MahaRERA Certificate of Competency, and promoters may list only certified agents. Source: https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/28615/maharera-issued-a-notification-regarding-the-final-extension-of-timeli/
- **Commission rates.** No commission rate is prescribed by the RERA Act or the Maharashtra rules; commission is contractual.

## 12. Other useful RERA timelines

All from the RERA Act.

- **Defect liability.** s.14(3): defects reported "within a period of five years ... from the date of handing over possession" must be rectified "without further charge, within thirty days".
- **Conveyance.** s.17(1) proviso: where no local law applies, the conveyance deed is executed "within three months from date of issue of occupancy certificate".
- **Taking possession.** s.19(10): the allottee must take possession "within a period of two months of the occupancy certificate".
- **Promoter penalty.** s.61: up to 5% of estimated project cost for other contraventions.
